## What we're building
A Solana memecoin whose trading fees fund group "breaks" of Collector Crypt gacha packs. On the website, players buy paid spots in USDC; some spots in every break are **free for coin holders**, drawn by verifiable randomness. At the deadline the keeper buys one pack per spot, pulls them all, and the pulled cards are randomly assigned across the spots and sent to the winners' wallets. Winners keep a card or sell it back on Collector Crypt. Everything runs automatically: break scheduling, draws, pack buying, delivery, website, and an X bot.

### Problems this design fixes (vs. the old model)
1. Big breaks stalled when fee volume was low → fees accumulate in a **treasury reserve**; breaks are only created when the reserve can fund them.
2. Breaks stayed open for hours waiting to fill → **fixed deadline**; an unfilled paid spot is simply not bought, so the pool is smaller. Nothing ever waits.
3. Non-payers had no way in → **free holder spots** in every break.

## Core rules (product decisions — don't change without asking)
- **Chain:** Solana. Programs in Anchor (Rust). Off-chain in TypeScript.
- **Fees:** launchpad creator fees land in a Squads multisig; the keeper sweeps them into the treasury program, which splits them into buckets:
  - `subsidy` (default 70%): tops up the cost of paid spots (if `spot_price < pack_price`)
  - `holder` (default 30%): pays for free holder spots
  - Split is a config value set by admin (multisig).
  - Fees arrive in SOL; the keeper swaps to USDC (Jupiter, slippage cap, per-sweep size cap) before depositing. The treasury is USDC-only.
- **Breaks are fee-funded from the first one.** No team pre-funding. A break can only be created when the buckets can cover its worst case (all paid spots fill, so every pack in its list is bought): the holder bucket reserves `free_spots × ⌊S ÷ n⌋` and the subsidy bucket the rest, `S − paid_spots × spot_price − free_spots × ⌊S ÷ n⌋`, where `S` is the sum of the pack prices in the list and `n = paid_spots + free_spots`. With one price for every pack this is the old `free_spots × pack + paid_spots × (pack − spot_price)`. The first break auto-opens as soon as the reserve crosses that threshold; the second the same way. The site shows a public progress bar ("Break #1 unlocks at $X — currently Y%"). With no vault or inventory to buy first, break #1 unlocks at pack cost, not prize value.
- **Break shape:** `paid_spots` (max, e.g. 10), `free_spots` (e.g. 2–4), `spot_price` in USDC, the **pack list** (next bullet), `opens_at`, `closes_at` (1–6 h window on devnet; the limits are set in `initialize`).
- **Pack list (decided with the owner on 2026-10-15; replaces "one machine and one price per break"):** a break lists up to 8 *tiers* (a Collector Crypt machine and the most one pack from it may cost) and, for every spot, paid and free together (at most 64), the tier its pack comes from. The list is committed at creation and is **cheapest first**: the program refuses a list that goes down in price, has a pack per spot too few or too many, or leaves a tier unused. The price of the top pack decides how big a break is (the owner's rough examples, not config: 2 grails at $1,000 and 13 cheaper packs for 15 spots with 2 free; about 20 spots with a $2,500 mythic; about 25 spots with 3 free, and a higher spot price, with a $5,000 legendary). Fees must not carry the whole break: they pay the free spots and top up the paid ones.
- **Which packs are bought:** the first `pool_size` of the list, so a break that does not fill drops its most expensive packs (the chase is unlocked by filling the break).
- **No markup, for the pack list:** the spot price may not be above the average price of the packs (rounded down), checked on the whole list when the break is created and again on the packs that will be bought when it is funded. A break whose bought packs average less than the spot price is not funded (`PacksWorthLessThanSpots`): it is cancelled and its spots refunded. **Anyone can cancel it** (`cancel_unfundable`, see the programs): a break that cannot be funded has no other way out, so it never waits for the admin. The same goes for a break nobody joined (`EmptyPool`). Any other break stays the admin's call (`cancel_break`, before funding).
- **Who pays for what:** a free spot is paid by the holder bucket at the average price of the bought packs (rounded down), a paid spot by its buyer at the spot price, the subsidy bucket pays the rest of the pack budget; rounding dust above the subsidy lock is taken from the holder lock (`programs/breaks/src/math.rs`, with a test that reaches it).
- **At the deadline:** pool size = filled paid spots + free spots (fewer if too few holders are eligible). The keeper buys exactly one pack per spot and pulls them. Unfilled paid spots are not bought. No house fills.
- **Shared pool:** all pulled cards go into one pool and are randomly assigned across the spots. This stops the keeper from steering a good pull to a chosen wallet. Spots are ordered: paid by purchase order, then free spots in draw order.
- **Pack randomness is Collector Crypt's.** Each pull uses Collector Crypt's on-chain VRF (per Jupiter's docs, with a per-pull proof). Our randomness covers only the free-spot draw and the card-to-spot assignment.
- **No staking (for now).** Free-spot eligibility = holding, measured by **time-weighted average balance (TWAB) over the break window** (`opens_at` → `closes_at`).
  - Minimum TWAB to be eligible (e.g. 1M tokens — config).
  - Weight = TWAB, **capped per wallet** (config) so whales can't dominate and splitting wallets gives no edge.
  - Exclude: LP pools, exchange wallets, team wallets, flagged bundle/sniper wallets. Exclusion list is public.
  - Staking may be added later as an extra weight multiplier — keep the eligibility module pluggable.
  > PENDING APPROVAL (A2): the cap rewards splitting (10× the cap in one wallet = 1× weight; split into ten wallets = 10×). Proposal: linear weight (no cap or a high sanity cap), keep the min TWAB, one spot per wallet, and merge wallets funded from the same source into one entity (published, with reason codes).
- **Randomness: ORAO VRF** (decided by the owner). One request per break, after `closes_at`, and **after both the eligibility root and the pool hash are committed**. See "Randomness" below. (Switchboard was dropped: news reports say it shut down on 2026-09-25.)
  > PENDING APPROVAL (A4): add a `challenge_period` (30–60 min) between `commit_eligibility` and `request_randomness`. The multisig may void the root (max 2 re-commits) while no seed exists yet.
- **Everything verifiable:** the pack list (machines, prices and order) committed at creation, eligibility root and pool hash committed before the draw, randomness proof stored, all derivations deterministic. The site shows a per-spot ledger: pack purchase tx → Collector Crypt VRF proof → assignment → delivery tx.
- **Known trust gap (MVP):** the chain cannot prove the keeper really bought every pack or delivered every card. The per-spot ledger makes any gap publicly visible. A tighter option is listed in Phase 0.

## Draw algorithm (deterministic, documented on the site)
Implemented in `draw-core/` (pure Rust, shared by the `breaks` program and its tests). `draw-core/vectors.json` holds a fixed example with every output; the TypeScript keeper and the "verify this draw" tool must reproduce it byte for byte (the Rust test fails when the file is stale).
- **Hash:** SHA-256 everywhere (the Solana syscall on-chain).
- **Eligibility tree (Merkle sum tree):** leaves are `(wallet, weight)`, sorted by wallet bytes ascending, strictly increasing (no wallet twice), weight > 0. Leaf hash `H(0x00 ‖ wallet(32) ‖ weight u64 LE)`, sum = weight. Inner hash `H(0x01 ‖ left.hash ‖ left.sum u64 LE ‖ right.hash ‖ right.sum u64 LE)`, sum = left + right (overflow = invalid). An odd node at the end of a level is carried up unchanged (never duplicated, which would count its weight twice). The root hash is committed in `commit_eligibility`, with `total_weight` = root sum. A proof is a list of `(sibling hash, sibling sum, sibling_is_left)`; it proves the leaf's range `[start, start + weight)` in `[0, total_weight)`, where `start` is the sum of the left siblings. So the keeper cannot hand-craft ranges.
- **Free-spot winners, drawn without replacement.** Winner `i` (0, 1, …): `remaining = total_weight − Σ weights of earlier winners`; `r = u128_le(H("breaks:free:v1" ‖ seed ‖ i as u16 LE)[0..16]) mod remaining`; then walk the earlier winners' ranges in ascending order of start and do `if r >= start { r += weight }`; the winner is the leaf whose range contains `r`. This always yields `min(free_spots, eligible)` distinct wallets however skewed the weights are (this replaces the earlier "retry with a nonce, give up after 32 tries" rule, which could fail when one wallet holds almost all the weight, after the pool size was already fixed).
- **`claim_free_spot(index, wallet, weight, proof)`** is permissionless (the keeper submits it) and **in order**: the program recomputes where winner `index` lands, checks the proof against the committed root and total weight, checks the wallet's range contains the drawn position, and records the winner. A wallet that appears in two leaves of a bad tree is refused the second time. When the last winner is recorded the break is `Drawn`; with no free winners it is `Drawn` as soon as the seed is final.
- **Card → spot assignment** (off-chain, anyone can recompute): the pool sorted by card mint (so pull order can't matter); spots are ordered paid spots by purchase order, then free spots in draw order. Fisher–Yates from the back: for `k` from `n−1` down to 1, `j = u128_le(H("breaks:items:v1" ‖ seed ‖ k as u16 LE)[0..16]) mod (k+1)`, swap positions `k` and `j`. Spot `s` gets the card at index `perm[s]` of the sorted pool.
- The eligibility commit also includes `inputs_hash` of the published `balance_events` snapshot, so anyone can recompute TWAB.
- Limits: at most 16 free spots per break (the winner list is stored in the break account); a proof is at most 40 steps, in practice about 14 for thousands of holders, which fits a transaction.

## Randomness

- **Provider:** ORAO VRF, program `VRFzZoJdhFWL8rkvu87LpKM3RbcVezpMEc6X5GVDr7y` (same address on mainnet and devnet). ORAO's Rust crate targets Anchor 0.32, so we do not use it: `programs/breaks/src/randomness.rs` builds the `request_v2` instruction by hand and reads the request account's bytes. Both layouts are pinned by tests against fixtures generated with the real `orao-solana-vrf` 0.7.0 crate.
- **Flow:** `request_randomness` (seed = `sha256("breaks:randomness:v1" || break || attempt || eligibility_root || pool_hash)`) → ORAO's oracles write 64 bytes (an Ed25519 signature of the seed) into the request account → `settle_randomness` stores `sha256("breaks:seed:v1" || those 64 bytes)` as the draw seed. Anyone can check the 64 bytes against ORAO's public key.
- **Why the seed is safe:** it cannot be known before the eligibility root and the pool hash are committed, so nobody can prepare an answer for it. If a stranger asks ORAO for the same seed first, `breaks` adopts that request.
- **Cost:** ORAO charges a request fee in SOL (its network state may also offer a fee in its token; the keeper does not use that). The keeper wallet pays it, plus the rent of the request account (about 0.0061 SOL). The fee and the treasury it goes to are read from ORAO's network state (see the Keeper section), never from configuration. The `breaks` program can pass ORAO's token-fee accounts as extra accounts, but nothing in the keeper uses that path.
- **Tests:** `programs/mock-orao` is a TEST-ONLY stand-in with ORAO's `request_v2` interface and account bytes, where anyone can fulfil with any value. It is never deployed with real money. `programs/breaks/tests/breaks.rs` runs everything against it. `programs/breaks/tests/real_orao.rs` runs the same flow against the **real ORAO program**: `scripts/dump-orao.sh` dumps ORAO's program and network state from devnet (CI step "Dump ORAO from devnet": it falls back to the dump of the last good run, kept in the Actions cache, with a warning when devnet cannot be read), the tests load both into LiteSVM and replace the list of oracles in the network state with one test key. That key's real Ed25519 signature of the seed is the answer (an Ed25519 instruction in front of `fulfill_v2`, checked by LiteSVM's Ed25519 precompile, so `litesvm` has the `precompiles` feature). Six tests (`#[ignore]`d, so a plain `cargo test` skips them; CI runs them with `--ignored`, and they fail on purpose when the dump is missing) cover: the request the breaks program builds is accepted, ORAO's fee reaches the treasury its network state names and its request counter moves; the oracle's signature becomes the draw seed and the free spots are claimed through to `Drawn`; answers by a key that is not an oracle, for another message, for another client, without a signature or with a forged signature are refused, each for its own reason pinned to the error (ORAO 6005 `UnauthorizedFulfillmentAuthority`, 6006 `InvalidFulfillMessage`, 6007 `MissingEd25519SigVerifyInstruction`, Anchor 2003 on `client`, and a precompile failure before ORAO runs); a request a stranger made first is adopted; a repeat after the timeout ignores the old answer; an answered request cannot be replaced and ORAO takes no second answer. Facts seen against devnet's ORAO on 2026-10-14: the request account is 749 bytes, the fee is 300,000 lamports (0.0003 SOL) to the treasury in the network state, there are 4 oracles and no token fee, and with one oracle the stored randomness equals that oracle's signature. The Ed25519 and `fulfill_v2` instructions, the network-state edit and the discriminators are also compared offline with bytes of the real `orao-solana-vrf` crate (`tools/orao-fixtures` prints them; `keeper/test/fixtures/orao.json`). **Not covered:** ORAO's real oracles and their combination of several answers (the tests use one), devnet's network state and program may differ from mainnet's, and the program upgrade authority. The tests need a reachable devnet (or a cached dump) and will notice when ORAO changes its errors or layouts.
- **Trust left:** ORAO's oracle set (a Byzantine quorum of its authorities) and the ORAO program upgrade authority. The admin can switch to another program with the same interface via `set_randomness`.

## Architecture

### 1. On-chain programs (`programs/`)
**`treasury`**
- `initialize(config)` — admin = Squads multisig; only the program's upgrade authority can call it.
- `deposit_fees(amount)` — splits into `subsidy` / `holder` buckets by config ratio.
- `set_split(subsidy_bps, holder_bps)` — admin only.
- `set_controller(controller)` — admin only. The controller is the `breaks` program's `treasury_authority` PDA.
- `lock_funds` / `unlock_funds` / `pay_from_locked` — controller only. Locking is how the on-chain funding rule works (the treasury refuses unless the bucket has that much unlocked). Pay-outs can only come out of locked funds, so they never touch money reserved for another break.
- Read-only balances for the website: the two vault balances and `locked_subsidy` / `locked_holder` in the config account.

**`breaks`** (built: the whole on-chain flow up to the recorded draw)
- `initialize(params)` — upgrade authority only. Config: admin, keeper, buyer wallet, window and spot limits, pause.
- `set_keeper`, `set_buyer`, `set_paused` — admin only. Pausing stops new breaks, new spots and `fund_purchases`; cancel and refunds always work.
- `create_break(params)` — keeper; takes the pack list (`tiers`: up to 8 × machine + `max_price`; `packs`: the tier of each pack, one per spot, cheapest first; checked by `packs::check_list`) and locks the worst case in the treasury (the funding rule, see "Core rules"). `spot_price ≤` the average pack price is enforced: a markup is not supported yet. The `Break` account is 1,418 bytes (8 tier slots of 40 bytes and 64 pack slots, whatever the break uses).
- `buy_spot(index)` — USDC into the break's escrow; one spot account per spot, `index` must be the next free one.
- `close_break` — anyone, after `closes_at`.
- `commit_eligibility(root, total_weight, entry_count, inputs_hash)` — keeper, after close.
- `fund_purchases` — keeper. Pays the prices of the first `pool_size` packs of the list (`pack_budget`) to the buyer wallet (escrow + locked subsidy + locked holder funds) and returns the unused locks; refuses with `PacksWorthLessThanSpots` when the bought packs average below the spot price. The destination is fixed to the configured buyer wallet. **This is the step users trust.** `pool_size` = sold paid spots + `min(free_spots, entry_count)`; an empty pool cannot be funded and is cancelled instead.
- `commit_pool(pool_hash, pool_size)` — keeper, after funding; `pool_size` must match.
- `cancel_break` — admin, before funding; `refund_spot` — anyone, pays the spot's owner.
- `cancel_unfundable` — **anyone, and no account signs it** (the sender only pays the fee). Only once the eligibility list is committed (the point where `fund_purchases` is next and the pool is known), and only while `fund_purchases` would refuse for good: nobody joined (`EmptyPool`), or the packs that would be bought are worth less on average than the spots cost (`PacksWorthLessThanSpots`). The rule is one function, `math::unfundable`, built on the same `plan_pool` that `funding` starts with, so the two can never disagree (a unit test sweeps every shape and checks it). Works while the program is paused (cancelling and refunding always work). A break that can be funded is refused with `BreakIsFundable`, and only the admin can still call it off. Releases the locked treasury funds exactly as `cancel_break` does (one shared function); buyers then take their money back with `refund_spot`. The new error is appended at the end of the enum, so the numbers of the others do not move. Tested by unit tests of `math::unfundable` (every reason, the exact edge, a nobody-paid break, the errors that are not reasons to cancel, and a sweep over every shape of the existing funding sweep that checks `unfundable` and `funding` always agree and fail for the same reason), by on-chain tests in `programs/breaks/tests/breaks.rs` that run in CI (a stranger cancels a break whose packs are worth less than its spots and every buyer is refunded in full; a break nobody joined; a fundable break is refused with `BreakIsFundable`, including the exact edge; every other status is refused; it works while paused; a sweep over 7 × 4 shapes of the mixed break that checks the cancel works exactly when funding would refuse and that every other shape is then funded), and by a vector (`breaks.cancel_unfundable`: six accounts, none a signer). Deliberate breaks: 11 in the Rust maths, all caught by the unit tests; the handler itself (`cancel.rs`: the status check, the `BreakIsFundable` check, the unlock) can only be broken on chain, so it is covered by the CI job `programs` and **not** by anything that runs in a cloud session.
- `request_randomness` — keeper, after the pool is committed. Asks ORAO for randomness with a seed that commits to the break, the attempt, the eligibility root and the pool hash. The keeper pays ORAO's request fee. If someone already asked ORAO for the same seed, the request is adopted instead of failing. A request that ORAO never answers can be repeated after `randomness_timeout_secs` (at most 5 attempts), but never once it has been answered.
- `settle_randomness` — anyone, once ORAO has answered: stores the seed of the draw (`sha256("breaks:seed:v1" || ORAO's 64 bytes)`). Final.
- `set_randomness(program, timeout_secs)` — admin only: which VRF program to use (ORAO's address in production) and the repeat timeout.
- `claim_free_spot(index, wallet, weight, proof)` — anyone, once the seed is final; records the free-spot winners in order (see "Draw algorithm"). Status flow: … `PoolCommitted` → `RandomnessRequested` → `Settled` → `Drawn`.
- Next: the keeper side of the pipeline (pack buyer, delivery). Open: what happens to buyer-wallet money that is not spent (return path), and the partial-failure policy.

No vault program. Cards pass through the buyer wallet. An on-chain escrow for cards is only added if Phase 0 shows it is possible.

### 2. Keeper (`keeper/`, TypeScript, runs on a server)
**Built so far (tested; wired into up to nine jobs with mock providers; never run against a real node):**
- `src/draw/`: TypeScript port of `draw-core` (sum tree, free-spot draw without replacement, card assignment). Tests reproduce every case in `draw-core/vectors.json` generated by the Rust code, and fail if either side changes.
- `src/eligibility/twab.ts`: TWAB from the indexed token transfers. An owner's balance at time `t` is the sum over its token accounts of the balance after each account's last event at or before `t`; TWAB = integral of that over the window divided by the window length (whole token base units, rounded down, exact bigint math). The opening balance of an account is `balance_after − delta` of its first event, so a history that starts mid-way works; a history where `delta` does not lead to `balance_after` is rejected. Events of one token account in one slot are put in the order that chains (`chainOrder`), since the data does not say which transaction ran first. What it makes of a set of fixed and random histories is pinned in `docs/twab-vectors.json` (`test/twab-vectors.test.ts`; `UPDATE_TWAB_VECTORS=1` rewrites it), which the website's own implementation is tested against.
- `src/eligibility/eligibility.ts`: who is eligible and with what weight (minimum TWAB, optional cap per wallet = pending decision A2, public exclusion codes, program-derived addresses excluded by default), the sorted leaves for the tree, the rows for `twab_snapshots` with a reason for every wallet that is out, and `inputs_hash`.
- `inputs_hash` = `sha256` of the text `breaks:inputs:v1,<window start>,<window end>` followed by one line per balance event in chronological order (`signature,slot,time,owner,tokenAccount,delta,balanceAfter`), each line ending in `\n`. That text is what gets published next to the eligibility list.
- `src/draw/pool.ts`: `pool_hash` = `sha256("breaks:pool:v1" ‖ mint_0 ‖ mint_1 ‖ …)` over the pool sorted by mint (32 bytes each, no duplicates), and `assignCards` (spot → card, paid spots first, then free spots in draw order).
- `src/solana/pubkey.ts`: base58, on-curve check, and PDA derivation checked against the real Solana values for every PDA the programs use.
- `src/solana/`: instruction builders and account decoders for both programs (`treasury.ts`, `breaks.ts`; Borsh `codec.ts`; shared `instruction.ts`). A builder returns `{programId, accounts: [{pubkey, isSigner, isWritable}], data}` and derives every PDA itself; the caller only passes wallets, token accounts and the two program ids (`Programs`). `breaks.ts` also has `requestId`, `drawSeed`, the ORAO request address and `decodeRequest` (same checks as the program), and decoders for `Break`, `BreaksConfig`, `Spot` and the treasury `Config`.
- **Instruction vectors:** `programs/<name>/instruction-vectors.json` is generated by the Rust tests (`programs/<name>/tests/instruction_vectors.rs`, no SBF build needed; `UPDATE_VECTORS=1 cargo test -p treasury -p breaks --test instruction_vectors` rewrites them, a plain run fails when they are stale). `keeper/test/instructions.test.ts` rebuilds every case (data bytes, account order and signer/writable flags) and decodes every sample account, and fails on a vector it has no builder for. Change an instruction or account in Rust → regenerate the vectors → the TypeScript test shows what to change.
- `src/packs/`: the pack buyer, written against the `PackProvider` steps (`findExisting`, `prepare`, `submit`, `status`, `reveal`; `provider.ts`) because the real Collector Crypt purchase flow is unknown until Phase 0 is done. `buyer.ts` (`PackBuyer.buyBreak`) runs one pack per index through `planned → submitted → confirmed → pulled` and keeps its notes in a `PackStore` (`store.ts` in memory, `sql-store.ts` over `packs` + `pack_attempts`, migration 0002; one contract test runs against both). The rules that make a double-buy impossible, each pinned by a test that fails when the rule is removed:
  - the attempt (signature and expiry) is saved BEFORE the purchase is sent (`startAttempt` is a compare-and-set, so two workers cannot both buy an index);
  - a new purchase is built only when the saved one reverted or is `expired` (provider says: past its expiry and certainly never landed; otherwise `pending`), and only after the wallet history was searched for the index's memo (`packMemo`);
  - the provider's price is re-checked against the pack's own cap (its tier's `maxPrice`, not the break's highest); a live price above the cap buys nothing and the pack waits;
  - a pack bought twice is reported (`BreakProgress.anomalies`, `PackRow.lastError`), never hidden; a pack that reverts `maxAttempts` times is `failed` and the buyer does nothing more with it (the partial-failure policy is still open).
  - `MockPackProvider` is a pretend chain (signatures before sending, block-height expiry) with injectable failures: `drop`, `throw`, `land-then-throw`, `revert`. `packBuyerJob` wraps the buyer for the runner; it needs a `BreakSource` (funded breaks → `BreakPlan`, progress report), which the pipeline provides.
- `src/delivery/`: sends each pulled card to the wallet the draw assigned it to, written against the `CardTransfer` steps (`ownerOf`, `prepare`, `submit`, `status`; `transfer.ts`) because how a Collector Crypt card moves (pNFT rule set, Core asset, whether it can still be sold back) is a Phase 0 answer. Same shape as the pack buyer (`deliverer.ts`, `store.ts` in memory + `sql-store.ts` over `deliveries` + `delivery_attempts`, migration 0003, one contract test for both stores, `MockCardTransfer`, `deliveryJob`). A wrong recipient cannot be undone, so:
  - `plan.ts` (`buildDeliveryPlan`) works out who gets which card only from what the chain committed, and throws a `PlanError` naming the first thing that does not add up: break not `Drawn`, empty seed, paid spots that do not match `spots_sold` (order, break, refunded), free winners that do not match `pool_size − spots_sold`, a pool that is not exactly the committed size, or whose `poolHash` is not the one committed before the draw. The assignment is `assignCards` (the verified Fisher–Yates), so the order the cards were pulled in does not matter.
  - the chain is the source of truth for where a card is: nothing is sent unless the buyer wallet owns it; a card already at its recipient counts as delivered (`adopted`, no signature); a card in any other wallet stops that delivery and is reported in `DeliveryProgress.anomalies`; a transfer reported confirmed whose card is not at the recipient is a failure, not a success;
  - the transfer the provider built is checked against the plan (recipient, card, sender) before anything is saved or sent; the attempt is saved before the send; a new transfer is built only after the saved one reverted or can never land;
  - if the plan for a spot ever differs from the recorded one (`PlanChangedError`), that delivery stops.
  - Not done: tracking Collector Crypt's sellback deadline (about 72 h from the pull) waits for Phase 0 to confirm it; the `deliveries.sellback_deadline` column stays empty.
- `src/pipeline/`: drives a break from the end of its sale to the draw being recorded, and connects the pack buyer and the deliverer to the chain. `pipeline.ts` (`BreakPipeline.advance`) starts every call from the on-chain `Break` status, never from notes of its own, so it is safe to call as often as you like: `Open` → `close_break` (5 s after `closes_at`, so a keeper clock ahead of the chain's does not waste a transaction), `Closed` → `commit_eligibility` with what the eligibility planner returns (or wait for it), `EligibilityCommitted` → `fund_purchases` only when the program is not paused, the pool is not empty and the packs that would be bought (the first `pool_size` of the list) cost at least the spot price on average (otherwise it sends `cancel_unfundable`, which anyone may, instead: the program would refuse to fund it, and it has no other way out; this is checked before the pause, since cancelling works while paused), the stored eligibility list is exactly the committed one (the free winners are claimed from it, so money does not move without it) and the buyer wallet's USDC account exists; `Funded` → `commit_pool` (only when every pack is pulled, no pack failed, the pool has exactly `pool_size` distinct cards and the buyer wallet holds each of them), `PoolCommitted` → `request_randomness` (seed = `requestId(break, attempt, root, pool_hash)`), `RandomnessRequested` → `settle_randomness` once ORAO answered, or a repeat (attempt + 1, previous request passed) after `randomness_timeout_secs` and never more than 5 attempts and never once answered, `Settled` → `claim_free_spot` one winner at a time in order. Before claiming it rebuilds the tree from the stored eligibility list and refuses to go on unless the root, total weight and entry count equal what was committed, and it checks that winners already recorded are the ones the draw gives. A send whose result is unknown is not repeated for `resendAfterMs`. - **Eligibility planner** (`src/eligibility/planner.ts`, migration 0005): from the indexed token balances to the values `commit_eligibility` takes, with the public record of how. `SqlEligibilityPlanner.plan` (1) returns the stored list if there is one; (2) otherwise waits until the balance indexer has read `INDEXER_SETTLE_SECS` past the end of the window (`indexer_state`; a quiet token has no events to show progress, so the indexer must record it itself), never computing from a history that may still grow; (3) takes `balance_events` before the window end and the active `exclusions`, builds the list (`buildEligibility`, `MIN_TWAB`, optional `WEIGHT_CAP` = pending A2), publishes the full record (every wallet's TWAB, weight and reason, and the exact balance-event text that `inputs_hash` commits to) through a `Publisher` (`DbPublisher`: `published_documents` addressed by SHA-256, URIs `db:sha256:<hex>`; IPFS/Arweave later behind the same port), and stores `twab_snapshots` and then `eligibility_lists` (the list row last, so its existence means everything before it was written). Every later call returns the stored values, so a retry commits exactly what was published even if the exclusions changed. `committed()` is a read-only lookup (used before funding). An empty list is a zero root, zero weight and zero entries (the program requires weight and count to be zero together). Assumes one keeper process drives a break.
- **Balance indexer** (`src/indexer/`, migration 0006): fills `balance_events` and `indexer_state` from plain JSON-RPC (`getSignaturesForAddress` and `getTransaction` with `jsonParsed`, finalized data only; `RpcTokenReader` works against any node or provider URL, nothing is vendor-specific). **How it stays complete:** tokens of a mint reach a token account only from the mint (mint-to) or from another token account of the mint, and every such transaction names both. So reading the mint and then every token account that shows up in what it reads, each from its first transaction (`TokenIndexer`), reads every transfer; there is no separate discovery step. It starts at the mint's first transaction, so it has to run from the token's launch (history is not skipped; a snapshot start could not be recomputed by others). `parse.ts` (`readTransaction`) turns a transaction into balance events from the node's pre/post token balances (exact base units; a closed account ends at 0, a new one starts at 0; a change of owner is two rows, which is why migration 0006 puts the owner in the unique key) and throws on what it cannot read exactly (a balance without an owner, no block time) instead of guessing. Work is cut into steps (one page of signatures of one address, `INDEXER_PAGE_SIZE`), each saved before the next in `indexed_accounts` (cursors `until_sig` / `resume_before` / `pass_newest`), within a call budget per run (`INDEXER_CALLS_PER_RUN`). The order inside a step is: note the accounts the page names, store its events, save the cursor, so a crash repeats work and never skips it (a stored transaction is not fetched again, so its accounts must be noted first). A finished pass sets `covered_until` = pass start − `INDEXER_LAG_SECS` (finalization lag and clock skew); `indexer_state.indexed_until` is the smallest `covered_until` over the accounts that may hold tokens, held while any of them has never been read, and never moving back. An account whose newest-slot balance is empty is left alone (`active = false`) until a transaction gives it tokens again. Every address is read again each `INDEXER_INTERVAL_SECS`, and at once when a break (status `open`/`closed`, no stored list) has a window end + `INDEXER_SETTLE_SECS` that is safely past. Cost: about one `getSignaturesForAddress` per holder per interval plus one `getTransaction` per token transaction; someone who spreads dust over many accounts adds accounts to read at their own SOL cost (not mitigated). `TOKEN_MINT` is required and `verifyMint` refuses to start unless it is an initialized mint (classic or Token-2022), since a wrong address would make every list empty.

- **Break scheduler** (`src/scheduler/`): creates the next break when the treasury can pay for its worst case. `BreakScheduler.run` starts every run from the chain: (1) every break the chain has and the database lacks is copied into `breaks` with the chain's values (a break made by an interrupted run, or by hand with the keeper key, is picked up; a database with no breaks takes all of them, otherwise only ids after its highest, at most 25 per run; a missing account below `next_break_id` is an error); (2) nothing is created while a break is still selling (`Open` and before `closes_at`, read from the chain, so a stale database cannot cause a second one); (3) the reserve is checked the way the program does it (`policy.ts` `worstCase`, the same as the program's `creation_locks`: vault balance minus `locked_*`, against what the break's whole pack list needs: the free spots at the average pack price from the holder bucket, the rest of what the buyers do not pay from the subsidy bucket; `reserveProgress` gives the percent for the public progress bar, and the job says it when it moves); (4) `create_break` is sent for exactly `next_break_id` with `opens_at` = now and `closes_at` = now + `BREAK_WINDOW_SECS`. The check comes after the reserve (a reserve that is short is waited for first, without nagging) and is skipped when the scheduler is built without a wallet reader (the program then refuses the break). The rent comes from constants, not from the node (it is the same on every cluster); the sizes are pinned to the Rust account vectors and the formula to values every Solana tool shows (an empty account 890,880, a token account 2,039,280). - **Fee sweep** (`src/sweep/`, migration 0007): fee SOL -> USDC -> the treasury. `FeeSweeper.run` has two steps, each safe to repeat. **Deposit:** whatever USDC the wallet's associated USDC account holds (at least `SWEEP_MIN_DEPOSIT`) goes into the treasury with `deposit_fees`; nothing is remembered, because a deposit that landed leaves nothing to deposit again and one that did not is simply sent again with the balance that is there (the token program refuses a second spend of the same USDC). At most one post per run. If a Collector Crypt service co-signs the purchase, `PackProvider.findExisting` (which searches for the memo `packMemo`) has to be redesigned.
- **Pipeline, still open:** run the keeper doctor first (`pnpm --filter keeper check-setup`), then a first run of the RPC client against a real node (devnet), including a send of each of the four instructions to the real program; the real `PackProvider` and `CardTransfer` (after Phase 0: create the recipient's token account, keeper pays the rent); and running the keeper's whole flow against the real programs (the on-chain side against the real ORAO is covered by `real_orao.rs`; the keeper itself is still only tested against its model of the programs). Priority fees and compute-budget instructions are not added (devnet). Cards must go out within minutes of the seed settling, because Collector Crypt's sellback window is about 72 hours from the pull.
- **Scheduler, still open:** the numbers in `BREAK_*` are placeholders until the owner decides them; breaks are only started when the previous sale has ended (no schedule by clock time). **Indexer, still open:** a first run against a real node (devnet) with a real token, and a real RPC provider's rate limits (`INDEXER_*` are guesses until then). The published inputs text lists every balance event since the token's launch and the planner loads them all, which is fine for thousands of holders but will need a smaller form (for example one opening balance per account at the start of the window) for a very busy token; if reading every holder gets too expensive, a streaming source (webhooks or Geyser) can sit behind the same `TokenReader` idea, but must keep the "every account that ever held tokens is read" guarantee.
- **X bot, still open:** confirm in X's developer console that OAuth 1.0a user keys can post, the plan and the price per post, and that the account can write; label the account as automated (an account setting). The results post gives counts, not "top hit → winner", because no card catalogue exists yet (Collector Crypt's card names and values are a Phase 0 question; `DeliveredFacts.topHit` is where it plugs in). `x_links` is filled by the website, which must check the wallet's signature before it inserts a row (the bot trusts the table, and tags only `may_tag` rows with a valid handle). "Final" for the results post is a time margin after the last delivery was confirmed (the deliverer reads the chain at the keeper's own commitment), not a finalized read of the cards. The bot assumes one keeper process.
- Every step safe to re-run.

**Buying a spot, step 2: connecting a wallet** (`web/lib/wallet/`, `web/lib/chain/balance.ts`, `web/app/wallet-*.tsx`): the "Connect wallet" button in the header, with nothing sent or signed. **Decision: the site talks to wallets through the Wallet Standard directly** (`@wallet-standard/app` finds the wallets a browser has, as Phantom, Solflare, Backpack and the others register themselves; the site's own code builds what a wallet signs (`lib/chain/buy.ts`). `wallets.ts` holds the rules as plain functions: a wallet is offered only if it can connect, can send Solana transactions **and supports `solana:devnet`** (`CHAIN`; **Buying a spot, step 3: the transaction and the purchase** (`web/lib/chain/{transaction,cluster,purchase}.ts`, `rpc.ts`): everything that happens between "the visitor clicked buy" and "the network confirmed", (the buy card of step 4 is what calls it). `rpc.ts` now has one JSON-RPC call (`rpc`) under every read (so every failure is said the same way) and `getAccounts` takes a `commitment`. **`cluster.ts` (`checkDevnet`) asks the node for its genesis hash and refuses to send anything unless it is devnet** (mainnet and testnet named, an unknown network refused, a node that cannot be asked refused: it fails closed), the same rule as the keeper's `cluster.ts`; a test checks the three hashes are the keeper's and are 32-byte addresses, **but all of them were written from memory: confirm with `solana genesis-hash -u devnet` (and the other two) before the first real run.** `transaction.ts` (`buildTransaction`) builds a legacy transaction from instructions, independently of the keeper's: accounts merged (signer or writable if any use says so), the fee payer first, signers before the rest, writable before read-only, each group in the order named, program ids last, the limits (1232 bytes, 255 accounts); it returns the message and the transaction with empty signature slots, which is what `solana:signAndSendTransaction` takes. It is pinned to web3.js's own bytes for the `buy_spot` instruction and for a case of merged flags by **comparing what the two messages say** (a test decoder, `test/support/message.ts`, itself checked against the Rust vector), not their byte order: web3.js sorts accounts inside a group by their text, which Solana does not need. `purchase.ts` (`buySpot`) runs a purchase: (1) the devnet check; (2) re-reads the config and the break **at `confirmed`** (not `finalized`, so the next spot is not an old one) and refuses a break that is missing, not the program's, another break's account, or not for sale (the same `checkSale` as the page); (3) re-reads the wallet's USDC at `confirmed` and its SOL, and refuses when it cannot pay the spot, or the **rent of the new spot account (asked of the node, 60 bytes, pinned to the Rust Spot vector), the 5,000-lamport fee and what a wallet must keep to stay a valid account**; (4) builds the transaction from a fresh blockhash; (5) only then asks the wallet to sign and send it (`chain: "solana:devnet"`). **The wallet sends it, not the page**, and the page does not trust the wallet's word: it asks its own node about the signature every 1.5 s (`getSignatureStatuses`, this transaction only) until `confirmed` or `finalized` (not `processed`), keeps asking through a node that fails now and then, says the transaction **expired** once the chain is past the last block its blockhash allowed (checked every fourth poll), and after 60 s says it is **unconfirmed** and may still land. The result says exactly how far it got: `refused` (a check failed: nothing was sent), `declined` (the wallet did not send it; the message tells the buyer to look at the wallet's history first, since a wallet can send and then fail to answer), `failed` (it reached the network and was refused, or expired: nothing was bought) and `unconfirmed` (do not try again before looking), `bought` (with the spot's index and price). The pages read at `finalized`, so a bought spot shows up a few seconds after `confirmed` (step 4 follows it). The sale window is judged by the visitor's clock (the program uses the chain's), so a wrong clock can refuse or allow wrongly: the wallet's own simulation is the backstop. **Refunding a spot** (`web/lib/chain/{refund,refund-state,run-refund,send}.ts`, `follow.ts`, `web/app/me/refund-status.tsx`): a **Refund $20** button on each spot of a cancelled break that has not been refunded, on the My spots page. It moves money, so it follows the buy card's rules, and a few more, since `refund_spot` is the least dangerous instruction there is: **no account signs it** (anyone can send a refund, the program always pays the spot's owner, and the sender only pays the network fee), and the USDC goes to a USDC account of the owner or the transaction fails. `refundSpotInstruction` is byte for byte the Rust program's own `breaks.refund_spot` vector (the break read-only, the escrow, the spot and the owner's USDC account writable, the token program). `checkRefund` repeats the program's checks in its order (the break is cancelled, then the spot is not refunded). `refundSpot` runs the flow: (1) the devnet check; (2) re-reads the break and the spot **at `confirmed`** and refuses what is missing, not the program's, unreadable or another break or spot; (3) **refuses a spot of another wallet** (the program would allow it, but this site only sends refunds for the connected wallet's own spots); (4) `checkRefund`; (5) reads the owner's USDC account and **refuses when it does not exist** (this transaction does not create one: the message says a USDC account is opened by receiving any USDC) or cannot receive (frozen, another token); (6) the wallet's SOL must cover the network fee and keep a valid account (no new account is created, so the rent of a spot account is not part of it); (7) builds the transaction from a fresh blockhash; (8) only then asks the wallet, and watches the network the same way as a purchase. **On the free-spot lists** (`web/lib/standing.ts`, `web/app/me/{standing-view,my-standing}.tsx`; `rowProblems` and `listProblem` split out of `checkRecord` in `web/lib/twab/record.ts`): a card on My spots that says where the connected wallet stands on each recent break's list, read from the keeper's published records (`records/<inputs hash>.json`; needs `NEXT_PUBLIC_PUBLISHED_URL`, and says so without it). It looks at the newest `STANDING_BREAKS` = 10 breaks that are not cancelled, out of the 50 My spots already reads (`Mine.recent`). A break on sale or just closed says its list is made after the sale ends. Every other break's record is fetched under the inputs hash on its account. A record is used only if **two checks** pass: `fetchRecord` finds that it states the break account's inputs hash, root, total weight, count and window, and `listProblem` finds that the wallets it puts on the list, hashed with the page's own tree, build the committed root, total and count. So whether this wallet is on a list, and with what weight, is the chain's word. **This wallet's own line** must then follow from the record's rules (`rowProblems`, the same checks the verify page runs on every wallet), and agree with the free-spot winners the chain recorded on the break. A winner that the record leaves out or puts off the list, or that has another weight, is refused in words. Each line reads one of these:
- **On the list**: average balance, weight and share of the list.
- **Not on the list**: below the minimum, left out on purpose (with the keeper's code), a program's account, or not in the record at all ("held none of the token before the sale ended").
- **Not known**, saying why: not published yet, could not be read, not what the chain committed, or does not add up. It links to the verify page for that break.

For a wallet on the list, the free spot reads as won (with its place in the draw), not drawn, being drawn, one for each wallet on the list (when there are no more wallets than free spots), or how many free spots will be drawn after the cards are pulled. A hint says a break at `EligibilityCommitted` can still be cancelled, and then gives no free spots. The average balance and the reason stay the keeper's word, and the card says so; the verify page checks them against the balance events.

**Checking only this wallet's line** is deliberate. Checking a whole record costs about 0.6 s per 5,000 wallets on the cloud sandbox's machine, mostly noble's SHA-256 at about 21 µs a hash, so a full check of ten big records would hold up a phone. The root check alone is about 0.23 s per 5,000. The records are fetched all at once, but checked one break at a time with a pause in between, so the page can draw. A record that passed both checks is kept for the visit (a module-level map in `my-standing.tsx`, keyed by everything the break commits), so Refresh and the quiet re-reads after a refund do not fetch it again. A missing or refused record is fetched again, and "Read the lists again" asks for those. `standingsKey` decides when to read again: the wallet, the breaks, their stage, their inputs hash and their winners. Switching wallet shows "Reading…" until the new wallet's standings are in, never the last wallet's.

**How it works** (`/how-it-works`, `web/app/how-it-works/page.tsx`; the first link of the navigation, and the home page's short version links to it): the plain-language page, static, nothing read from the chain. It follows one fee to a card in five steps (fees fill the reserve; a break opens when the reserve can pay its worst case, with no team money; paid and free spots, with holding measured as an average balance; at the deadline the lists are fixed on chain, packs are bought and pulled, and only then is randomness requested; the cards go to the spots, and a cancelled break's spots can be refunded), each step with a link to the page where it can be seen. Then what a visitor can check themselves (the treasury, each break, the verify page), **what they still have to trust** (the chain cannot prove the keeper bought every pack or sent every card, and a ledger that would show a gap is only planned; ORAO's oracles and whoever can upgrade its program; the card service decides what is in a pack, we only decide who gets which card) and a short list of words. **It is written from the decided rules and says out loud what is not decided or not built**, so it does not promise more than exists: a "test version" notice comes first (devnet, test money, packs and cards not connected to the real card service, some of it designed but not built), the rule for very large holders is "still being decided" (A2), the public list of left-out wallets "is meant to be public" (the exclusions are not published yet) and the per-spot ledger is shown on a break's page once its cards are all sent, with the transactions to look up. It uses no numbers that are still open decisions (spot price, spot counts, window, minimum average balance, the split). **Words that sound like a promise of returns are kept out on purpose** (a test fails on `guarantee`, `profit`, `invest`, `earn`, `returns`, `payout`, `jackpot`, `odds`, `prize` and the like), since the legal review is still open. Tested by `web/test/how.test.ts` (the title and sections in order, the five steps and their titles and one link each, the notice before the steps, the decided rules and the undecided ones in their words, what you still have to trust, the forbidden words, that every link points to a page that exists in `web/app`, the glossary, the navigation order and the home page's link) and `nav.test.ts`, by 28 deliberate breaks of the page, the navigation and the home link that the tests all catch (three of the first attempt only missed because a pattern did not match the source's line breaks), and in a real browser at 1100 px and 390 px (the page, the five-link navigation in two rows on a phone, every link on the page and the one from the home page, no sideways scroll, no console errors). Step 2 says a break names its packs when it is created, from one machine or several, and that list never changes; step 4 says packs are listed cheapest first and only as many are bought as there are spots, so a break that does not fill loses its most expensive packs, and that a break whose bought packs would be worth less than the spots cost on average is cancelled before anything is bought and refunded (`how.test.ts` asserts these sentences). If a rule in "Core rules" changes, this page is one of the places that has to change with it.

**Reserve bar** (`web/lib/chain/reserve.ts`, `web/app/reserve-card.tsx`, `web/app/next-break.tsx`; settings in `web/lib/config.ts` `breakShapeSetup`): how far the treasury is from paying for the next break, as a card at the top of `/treasury` and on the home page under the latest break; nothing is sent. `worstCase` is the program's funding rule over the whole pack list (Rust `creation_locks`: with `S` the sum of the pack prices and `n` the number of spots, the holder bucket needs `free × ⌊S ÷ n⌋` and the subsidy bucket `S − paid × spot_price − free × ⌊S ÷ n⌋`; it refuses a pack list that does not have one pack per spot, a spot price above the average pack price and amounts that do not fit in 64 bits) and `reserveProgress` is the keeper's (`policy.ts`: the bucket furthest from its target decides, rounded down, 100 only when both can pay, so a micro-dollar short reads 99%, never 100%). ## Run and test
```
pnpm install
pnpm typecheck
pnpm test                    # keeper tests (incl. db/migrations applied on an in-memory Postgres, PGlite) and web tests
pnpm --filter web build
```

CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs the same on every pull request and on pushes to main.
`keeper/vitest.config.ts` sets generous test and hook timeouts (30 s / 60 s): many test files start an in-memory Postgres, which takes seconds when all files run at once, and the default 5 s limit made the suite fail now and then on a busy machine.

**Vectors:** `cargo test -p treasury -p breaks --test instruction_vectors` checks the committed `instruction-vectors.json` files (see Keeper). **Program tests:** each program has pure-logic unit tests (`cargo test -p treasury -p breaks -p draw-core --lib`, runs anywhere) and on-chain tests in `programs/<name>/tests/` that run the compiled program inside LiteSVM. The on-chain tests need `target/deploy/<name>.so` from `cargo build-sbf --manifest-path programs/<name>/Cargo.toml`; without it they fail on purpose.

On-chain tests share helpers in `testkit/` (deploy with an upgrade authority, send, error and event assertions, clock). Both programs' tests need both `.so` files, so build both before `cargo test`. The `breaks` tests share one harness, `programs/breaks/tests/common/` (`Env`), which runs on `mock-orao` by default and on the real ORAO program with `Env::ready_with(Some(&RealOrao::load()))`. To run the real-ORAO tests yourself: `scripts/dump-orao.sh` (needs the Solana CLI, curl, jq and network access to devnet), then `cargo test -p breaks --test real_orao -- --ignored`.

**Toolchain limit in cloud sessions:** the Solana/Anchor binaries could not be downloaded there (release.anza.xyz and GitHub release downloads were blocked by the network policy), so `cargo build-sbf` and the on-chain tests run in CI (job `programs`), not in the session. Read CI results with the GitHub Actions tools. CI also runs on pushes to `claude/**` branches so work can be verified without opening a pull request. Do not claim on-chain behavior is tested until that job is green.

## Build phases
0. **Feasibility (no code, you run these; the first one gates everything):**
   1. Buy one pack from wallet A, send the card to wallet B, check that B can still sell it back.
   2. Check whether a pack can be bought so the card lands directly in another wallet.
   3. Check for a Collector Crypt / Jupiter Gacha API or terms for programmatic purchases.
   4. Check whether our program could verify a pull from Collector Crypt's on-chain accounts. If yes, release funds per verified pull and close the trust gap.
   5. Check whether the card can be held in a program-owned account (only if an on-chain escrow is wanted).
   6. (done) VRF provider: ORAO.

## Guardrails for Claude Code
- **Devnet only** until the user explicitly says otherwise after an audit. Collector Crypt is a mainnet service, so the pack buyer runs against a mock on devnet. Real Collector Crypt tests are manual and run by the user.
- Never commit private keys, seed phrases, or API keys. Use `.env` (gitignored) and `.env.example`.
- All admin/authority roles and program upgrade authority → Squads multisig, never a single hot wallet.
- **Exception: the buyer wallet.** It holds only the pack budget funded per break, up to the cap in `fund_purchases`, and has no authority over any program.
- Every program instruction gets tests for success and failure cases before moving on.
- Keeper steps must be idempotent and retry-safe, especially the pack buyer.
- Ask before changing any rule in "Core rules".

## Open decisions
